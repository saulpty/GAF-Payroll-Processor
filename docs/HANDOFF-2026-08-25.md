## 1. What changed today — weekend schedules

Tim reported that some employees are on a weekend schedule and asked what rules
the app has for them. The answer turned out to be: the rule existed, and nobody
was using it.

### The rule

`schedules.work_days` — a CSV of day abbreviations per schedule, default
`'Mon,Tue,Wed,Thu,Fri'`, added by migration `1781402000`. The classification
engine honours it at `classificationEngine.ts:462`: a non-scheduled day is
skipped silently, an off-day punch with no form is dropped, and an off-day with
a Monday form becomes YELLOW for operator review. Holidays still apply, because
the holiday check runs after the work-day check.

### What was wrong

Three weekend schedules were already defined and correct:

| id | Name | `work_days` |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Weekend Schedule Mon-Tue OFF | `Wed,Thu,Fri,Sat,Sun` |
| 11 | Weekend Schedule Tue-Wed OFF | `Mon,Thu,Fri,Sat,Sun` |
| 12 | Weekend Schedule Thu-Fri OFF | `Mon,Tue,Wed,Sat,Sun` |

**No employee was assigned to any of them.** All 50 sat on schedules 1/2/3. The
Schedules tab cannot assign anyone — it only defines schedules, while its
"pick an employee" datalist merely names one. Assignment lives in Admin →
Employees → Roster. That trap is now `BACKLOG.md` #10.

Consequence: the engine built Mon–Fri entries for four people who do not work
Mon–Fri, found no punches, and marked them `Ausencia Injustificada` / RED, while
discarding their real Saturday and Sunday work.

### Fixed today

**Assignment** (Saul, in Admin → Employees → Roster) — verified live:

|---|---|---|
| Cemiriamiz Iglesias | 46 | 11 |
| Euclides Gonzalez | 49 | 10 |

**Migration `1781900100`** — `v_attendance_daily` now filters on each employee's
own `work_days` instead of `WHERE EXTRACT(isodow FROM work_date) < 6`, so
weekend workers are no longer invisible on the Attendance dashboard. The `base`

## 6. Base hours — resolved by the owner, 2026-08-25

**The rule:** employees work **5 days a week, 8 hours a day, and are paid as if
they worked a 6th day.** That is Panama's paid weekly rest day.

**`loadHrkSummary.ts:30` already implements this correctly. Do not "fix" it.**

```sql
WHERE EXTRACT(DOW FROM d) BETWEEN 1 AND 6   -- Mon..Sat, i.e. every day except Sunday
```

The critical property is that **this calculation never looks at the employee's
schedule.** It counts non-Sunday calendar days in the period and multiplies by
8. So every employee is credited 6 paid days per week regardless of *which* 5
they actually work. A Wed–Sun employee and a Mon–Fri employee both get the same
base, which is exactly the intended policy.

Worked for `Q2-Aug-2026` (Aug 10–24):

| | |
|---|---|
| calendar days | 15 |
| Sundays excluded | 2 |
| paid days | 13 |
| **base hours** | **13 × 8 = 104** |

Matches the 104 shown for all 45 employees. Over a full year both patterns yield
365 − 52 = 313 paid days, so the two are equal in the long run.

**Do not thread `work_days` into `base_hours`.** Doing so would drop Saturday for
every Mon–Fri employee — roughly four days of pay a month — and would break the
rest-day rule for everyone. This was the earlier open question and the answer is
that the existing behaviour is right.

*Boundary note, not a defect:* within a single period the counts can differ
slightly — in Q2-Aug a Wed–Sun employee worked 10 days and a Mon–Fri employee 11,
while both were paid 13, because that window spans three Mondays and only two
Sundays. Inherent to slicing a 6-day-paid week into fortnights; it evens out.

### The one residual, and it is reporting-only

`loadHrkSummary.ts:44-54` still expands a Friday PTO day to include the following
Saturday and Sunday, hardcoding `EXTRACT(DOW) = 5`. For a Wed–Sun employee the
equivalent day is not Friday, so the wrong days get appended.

**This does not affect pay.** `total_worked_hours` is
`base − discount − incapacidad − constancia`; PTO is not subtracted. The
expansion only inflates the `pto_days` / `pto_dates` columns of the HRK export,
which are informational. Low priority, fix whenever PTO is next touched.

### Does it still hold for weekend employees? Checked properly, 2026-08-25.

Yes. Asked directly, so it was measured across all 11 periods on record rather
than argued from the shape of the formula.

Periods are **quincenas** — semi-monthly, 11th→25th and 26th→10th, so 13–17
calendar days. They never contain a whole number of weeks. A rest-day rule is
inherently weekly, so within any one period the "non-Sunday count" proxy drifts
from a strict 6-paid-per-5-worked.

**It drifts for everyone, not just weekend staff:**

## 8. FilterBar params-wrapper — fixed 2026-08-25 (commit `425b8a1`)

Third occurrence of the defect in `LESSONS.md`. `FilterBar.tsx:71` passed
`{ params: { periodName: period } }`; every other call site passes flat.

**Symptom was total silence.** `loadActionRequiredCounts` saw `{{params.periodName}}`
as NULL → `(NULL = '' OR period_name = NULL)` is NULL → `WHERE` matched nothing →
`SUM` over an empty set returned NULL → the render guard `{tabCount > 0 && ...}`
at `FilterBar.tsx:192` meant **the RED and YELLOW tab counts on Action Required
never appeared at all**, for any period. A feature that renders nothing looks
deliberate, which is presumably why it went unreported.

Fix is one line. Verified with three independent cross-checks:

| Period | RED | YELLOW | Sum | Corroborated by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All periods | 56 | 35 | 91 | nav badge (`loadUnresolvedCount`) reads 91 |
| Q2-Aug-2026 | 25 | 6 | 31 | Recent Periods card reads "31 open" |
| Q2-Jul-2026 | — | — | 0 | Recent Periods card reads "All clear" |

Those three numbers come from separate code paths, so they corroborate rather
than merely agree.

`grep -rn "{ params:" src/app` now returns nothing.

**Correction to something stated earlier in this session:** this bug was *not*
the cause of the Action Required nav badge reading 87/91. That badge is fed by
