# Handoff — 2026-09-08

Everything below is **released to prod** unless it says otherwise.

## What shipped

**Attendance → Reports tab** (`/attendance/reports`). One row per employee per
scheduled work day: the punches, the minutes late, the Absence/Tardiness form
behind the day, and whether that form was filed **before the shift started** —
the company rule. Weekly strips per person by default, toggling to a full
day-by-day table with verdict filters. Honours date range, employee, role and
manager filters.

**Unexplained absences now count.** They used to sit inside `Excused` alongside
PTO and holidays and were dropped from every attendance score, so a day nobody
could account for counted as neither good nor bad. They are their own status now
and count against the on-time rate. 29 rows moved across all history; on-time
rate shifted 59.5% → 59.1% over a 30-day window.

**"Excused" is now "Time off"**, broken down in the donut legend into PTO,
Holiday, Birthday/comp day and Approved absence, with zero-count categories
omitted. A tenth column, `time_off_kind`, was appended to `v_attendance_daily`
to make that possible.

**Every KPI explains itself.** Eight one-line tooltips on the KPI labels and the
At Risk badge. The At Risk one matters most: that badge is **on-time rate alone**
— it ignores absences and missing forms, which was not evident from a badge that
looks like an overall verdict.

**A Reporting column** on the Employees table: of the days that needed an
explanation (late or absent), how many had a form filed. Deliberately separate
from At Risk, because punctuality and rule-following are different behaviours.
Arelis Acosta reads *At Risk / Complete 8/8* — often late, always files. Alisha
Dua reads *Fair / Rarely 0/2* — the reverse. One badge would have hidden both.
An employee with nothing to report shows a neutral dash, never a red badge.

**`filed_gaf` no longer breaks on the next payroll run.** The engine started
writing `'Attendance Form'` on 2026-08-27 while the view still only knew
`'Form Submitted'`. It was correct purely by timing — the last processed period
ran two days before the rename. The next run would have silently zeroed
"Late — Reported". Migration `1781993000` accepts both, and
`tests/filedGafGuard.test.ts` now fails if the two ever drift again.

**Forms filed from an unrecognised email now reach their owner.** `rowMatchesEmp`
returned early whenever a row carried an email, so the alias and name paths were
unreachable and **113 forms across 24 employees were invisible to every payroll
run**. The email is now the first test, not the only test: an address matching
this employee wins; an address belonging to a *different* roster employee loses
outright; an address belonging to nobody falls through to name/alias.

Also released, having sat on `/dev` unreleased: **Contracts & milestone
tracking** (built 09-01) and the **Disciplinary actions viewer** (built 09-07).

## What the work found in the live data

- **47 of 97 late days since 2026-08-15 had no form.** Roughly half the time the
  policy is not followed. Not new — just never visible in one place.
- **113 forms invisible to payroll**, now fixed in code. 44 of them are one
  consistent wrong domain per person (Juan Molina: 34 forms), which is a **stale
  roster, not a typo** — and those people's *Teramind punches* still key off
  `teramind_email` with no name fallback, so **the addresses still need
  correcting at source.** See `docs/findings/2026-09-08-form-email-mismatch.md`.
- **The 13 "misclassified" docked days are not a bug.** The engine found every
  form, classified the day as justified, raised it YELLOW, and an operator
  recorded it as unjustified anyway. Proven via `auto_notes`, which the engine
  writes once and no update path touches: all seven unexplained rows carry Step
  3's *"Absence form. Reason: …"* text, not Step 5's *"NO DATA + NO FORM"*.
  Six days at 480 minutes across two people. **A review sheet is in
  `docs/findings/2026-09-08-seven-docked-days-review.md`** — this is an HR
  question, not an engineering one, and nothing was re-run or reversed.
- That investigation also corrected an earlier finding of mine: three of the six
  rows I had reported as "explained by the Tardiness gap" are **seed data** from
  the initial migration and were never engine output.

## Things that would have shipped broken

Recorded because each was caught by a specific check rather than by luck.

- **Removing `Ausencia Injustificada` from the excused list would have deleted
  29 rows, not moved them.** The view ends with
  `AND (entry_t IS NOT NULL OR is_excused OR is_permission)` and an absence has
  no entry time. Excused would still have read the expected number while the
  total silently dropped. Caught by verifying **total row count first**.
- **Absences carry `late_minutes = 0`**, so ordering the new status branch after
  the `WHEN late_minutes = 0 THEN 'on_time'` arm would have bucketed every
  no-show as punctual — worse than the bug being fixed.
- **The Reports tab first rendered every day for every employee as an absence** —
  440 absent, 0 on-time, nothing thrown, nothing logged. Two silent input
  mismatches: a Postgres `DATE` arrives as `2026-06-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and a
  `BIGINT` can arrive as a string. A missed `Map` lookup renders as *someone who
  did not come to work*. Fixed by normalising at the boundary, not query by
  query — the first attempt patched three queries and the page was still wrong.
  See `docs/LESSONS.md`.
- **`9:54 AM AM`.** Caught by Saul on the live app. The doubled suffix was the
  visible half; the real defect was that the meridiem was recomputed from a
  12-hour hour, so `1:30 PM` rendered as `1:30 PM AM`. Every afternoon punch
  from 1 PM was labelled AM.

### The review that ran before release

A full adversarial review of the day's work found **five correctness bugs**, all
confirmed against the code before fixing, all now covered by tests:

1. A form whose `submitted_at` carried no clock time was judged **filed late** —
   including a form filed two days early. `submitted_at` is TEXT written
   verbatim from Monday, and the sync itself notes it "may include time on some
   Monday column types".
2. An employee with **no payroll rows at all** in a processed period read as
   absent every scheduled day: 0% on-time and a month of absences for a new
   hire, or for anyone an operator excluded from a run.
3. Request types were matched **exactly** here and with a lowercased substring
   test in the engine — two matchers on one dataset. Not producing wrong data
   today, since the board's values match exactly; one stray space away from it.
4. A one-day request recorded with `return_date = start_date` covered **zero**
   days and became a scored unexplained absence.
5. Someone with **zero expected days was badged At Risk**, because `pctOnTime`
   is 0 when the denominator is 0. A person on PTO for the whole range was
   branded At Risk on one tab and shown a neutral dash on the other.

Plus a documentation defect: migration `1781995000`'s rollback note would have
**taken the Attendance page down** if followed, because `loadAttendanceDaily`
now selects the column that rollback drops.

**One of my own tests had to be revised, and it was a genuine contradiction.**
R12 originally asserted that *any* day without a payroll row is an unexplained
absence — using an employee with no rows at all, which is exactly the case R51
now says must **not** be an absence. Claiming absences for someone missing from
the data is the worse error, so R12 was narrowed to its real intent: a gap is an
absence when the employee is otherwise present in the data.

**208 tests pass**, up from 139 at the start of the day.

### Two things the review raised that are NOT fixed

Recorded deliberately rather than silently dropped:

- **`rosterEmails` is built from the payroll roster, not the full employee
  roster.** `loadEmployees` filters `active = TRUE AND excluded_from_payroll =
  FALSE`, so a departed employee's address reads as "belongs to nobody" and can
  fall through to the name path. Low probability, but it is the exact scenario
  the new matcher exists to guard, and no current test can catch it — the test
  harness builds `employees` by hand. Fix would be an explicit `knownEmails`
  on `EngineInput` fed by an unfiltered `SELECT teramind_email FROM employees`.
- **The `mondayAdjustments` board is untested** in `emailFallback.test.ts`. The
  other three boards each have a case; had the fourth call site been missed, all
  tests would still be green. The file's own header overstates its coverage.

Neither is a live defect. Both are worth closing before the matcher is trusted
in a wider context.
